From paper to slides

One article, three prompts, three decks. Then compare, keep the best slides, fix them one by one.

Open Gemini Notebook ↗ The article (PDF) ↗ DOI 10.5334/ah.65 ↗ Slides ↗ Example decks ↓

Jo'anne Van Ooijen, Resilient Matters: The Cathedral of Syracuse as an Architectural Palimpsest, Architectural Histories 7(1), 2019. Open access, CC BY.

1 · Add the article

  1. notebook.google.com with your @unipg.it account → Create new notebook.
  2. Add sources → Websites → paste the direct PDF link:
    https://journal.eahn.org/article/7590/galley/21444/download/
  3. Wait for the summary in the centre: if it talks about the Cathedral of Syracuse, the source is in.
With your own PDFs, do the 30-second check first: select a line, copy it, paste it. If it comes out garbled, the model will still answer, with something plausible.

2 · Launch three decks

Studio → Presentation opens the customise dialog. Every time:

  1. Format: Presenter slides (the right-hand card).
  2. Language: English.
  3. Paste the prompt → Generate. Close the dialog, don't watch the spinner.
Three presentations per account per day. The dialog forgets format and language every time: set them again. About 7 minutes each; launched together, they finish together.

4 · Check in chat, with the numbers

Chat · the claims
Using only the uploaded document, and citing the page for each point: 1. What is the research question? 2. What thesis does the author defend? Write it as one sentence with a verb. 3. What are the three or four claims the thesis rests on? 4. Which limits does the author state explicitly?

Click every number: the passage opens. If it says less than the sentence, that sentence does not go on a slide.

5 · Compare A, B, C: five questions

#Look for
1A picture of a real place or person the tool made up
2A number or date you cannot find in the article
3A title that is a topic instead of a claim
4A verb stronger than the text: suggests → proves. This is the one that matters.
5A theme that holds on the first slide only
Generated slides are images. The text is drawn: read it yourself. We found "Walls of the central Walls of the central nave" on a timeline this morning.

6 · Rivedi: one slide, one instruction

Open a deck → Rivedi → click a slide → one instruction → Generate revised presentation. It makes a copy "(2)", the original stays. About 2 minutes, and it does not count toward your three.

  • Shorten the title to at most 10 words, keeping its meaning. Keep layout, colours and text exactly as they are.
  • Replace the title with: "…". Keep layout and image.
  • Turn the bullet list into two short sentences. Keep the diagram.
  • Use the UNIPG blue #27348B for the title only.
Rivedi does not read the sources. It fixes how something is said. What is said is fixed in chat, with the citations open.

Export: ⋮ → Download → PDF. Export every version you like: you cannot rerun one.

Three rules

  1. The prompt is the theme file. Codes, not adjectives. Constraints, not wishes.
  2. Rivedi fixes form, the chat fixes content.
  3. Every deck is a draw. Export it, read every slide, keep the good ones.

Declare it when you use it: "Slides drafted with Gemini Notebook from the author's article, checked and edited by the author."
